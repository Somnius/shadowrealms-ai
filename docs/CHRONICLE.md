# The chronicle so far

Since 0.24. The chat of a chronicle written up as a story, night by night, by the local model, the way meeting minutes are written from a recording. The plan and the phases are in [ROADMAP_CHRONICLE.md](ROADMAP_CHRONICLE.md).

## Who sees it

| Who | What |
|---|---|
| The Storyteller (the chronicle's creator) and site admins | read, Update, Rewrite everything, Translate, export, and the edit copy (0.25) |
| Helpers who can open the chronicle (they are a member of it) | read and export only |
| Every member, players included | "Previously on…" in the play page (0.27): the player view of a room's recent nights, never the Storyteller's part, never the export |
| Anyone else | 404, as for the chronicle itself |

It lives in **Chronicle settings → The chronicle so far**.

## How it is written

1. **Lines.** Every message becomes one numbered line, `M1234 [21:05] [IC] Marcus: text`. The number is the message id, so every event can point back to where it came from. Dice markers, `/ai` helper lines and empty rows are left out; a dice result is one line with the roller's name. Usernames never appear in a line: a character speaks under its name, a player out of character as "Player", staff as "Storyteller" or "Staff".
2. **Chapters.** One room and one night: a run of messages in one room with no gap over 6 hours. A session-end mark (`chronicle_session_marks`, set from the message menu since 0.26, see below) ends the session in every room: each room is cut at the marked message's time. Rooms are mixed in time order on one timeline. Nights are numbered by the nights players can see, so the numbers are the same in every view and in the open threads; a closed room's night has no number.
3. **Two views.** The story of a night is written from what players could read: hidden rolls and closed rooms are left out (the same rules as the chat: `hidden_dice_sql_filter` and `readable_location_ids`). If an open room had hidden rolls, a second short call writes `st_notes` ("what the players didn't see") from those rows only. A night in a closed room is written whole but shown only in the Storyteller's view.
4. **Table talk.** The OOC lobby and out-of-character lines inside a story room are stored as they were (`ooc`), never shown to the model, and exported only "with table talk".
5. **The model's answer** is one JSON object per night, enforced with `response_format` (a JSON schema): a title, a summary of 2–4 paragraphs, events that cite message ids, characters, places, threads opened and closed. The instructions are in the chronicle's language (Greek instructions for a Greek chronicle; English ones gave English text even when asked for Greek), say that the chat is data and not instructions, and forbid clans, titles, offices, relationships and places that aren't in the lines.
6. **Checks.** Unknown ids are dropped, an event needs at least one id, a character must be a name the chronicle has. The text must be in the chronicle's language (at least half Greek letters for Greek, at most a fifth for English) and no word may mix alphabets. A failed check is asked once more, with the model's answer and the reason; letters of other alphabets that survive are removed. A night still in the wrong language is translated into the chronicle's language by the translator role, with names protected as chat translation does (`services/translation.py`), and marked `translated`. Only if that fails is it kept as written and flagged: the panel and the Storyteller's exports show "not fully in Greek" (or English) on that night.
7. **Botches and criticals** are always listed: any that the model didn't cite are added as one short sentence in the chronicle's language, built from the dice row's data with the read-out's outcome words («Κωνσταντίνος: κρίσιμη επιτυχία στη ρίψη Intelligence + Investigation.»). Message numbers the model writes inside a text ("(M283)") are removed; the citation list keeps them. A title that is just the room's name or in the wrong language is asked for once more on its own, then replaced by a fixed one («Νύχτα της 5 Οκτ 2026 στο …»). The spellings list holds the characters, NPCs and rooms (`translation.chronicle_names`); a name stored in both alphabets keeps only the chronicle's language form.
8. **Eras and the whole.** About 10 nights make an era digest; one final join over the eras (at most 8 at a time) writes "In short", "The story so far" and the open threads, plus a Storyteller-only part from closed rooms and `st_notes`.
9. **Only what changed.** Each night has a hash of what the model would see (its lines, its hidden rows, the names its lines mention, the language, whether the room is open). Update rewrites only nights whose hash changed and eras that hold them; nights that no longer exist (a session mark, a moved gap, deleted messages) are removed.

Nothing runs per message. Nothing is written below 8 story messages.

## The language

`campaigns.language` (`el` or `en`) when it is set; otherwise the language most story messages are in. Since 0.25 it is asked when a chronicle is made (the default is the creator's app language) and can be changed in Chronicle settings → Chronicle language (or set back to "most messages decide"); the backend takes only `el`, `en` or empty. The next Update writes in the new language.

**The other language (0.25).** The language switch in the panel shows the story in the other language. Translate (Storyteller and site admins) starts a job with `scope: translate` that goes through the same queue as Update: one job per chronicle, the quiet window, at most every 5 minutes, live progress ("Translating: text 3 of 40") and Cancel. Every text of the finished digest and of each written night (titles, paragraphs, events, threads, notes, the Storyteller part) goes through the `translator` role one at a time, with the chronicle's names hidden behind placeholders and put back, exactly as chat translation does (`services/translation.py`). A text longer than the translator's 2,000-character limit is cut at sentence ends into pieces that are translated and joined. Each call has a 15 s timeout and Cancel is checked before every call and retry. A text the translator can't do (an unusable answer, a timeout, a lost connection) stays as written and the job goes on; the job then ends `partial` and the panel says "3 texts are still in Greek" and offers Translate again. If none can be done the job fails with `translate_failed`. When the model drops a name placeholder, the retry keeps the names hidden (never in clear), for chat translation too. A translation never replaces a written digest that carries an edit copy (left from before the chronicle's language changed): Translate answers 409 `edited_in_target` and the panel says so. Translations have their own 5-minute window and never hold Update back. The result is cached as its own `chronicle_digests` row in the other language, marked `translated_from`, with a hash of the digest and nights it was made from: when the story is written again the panel says the translation is behind and offers Translate again. The writer never reads a translated row as its own digest. The per-night fallback above (a night that came out in the wrong language) is a different thing. Greek is written by the `chronicler_el` role (default Krikri), English by `chronicler_en` (default: the loaded LM Studio model); both are in Admin → AI system and never fall back to the small Ollama model.

## Running it

- **Update** and **Rewrite everything** are for the Storyteller and site admins. Both first show how long it will take: (nights to write + the eras that change + the join) × the median seconds per call of the last 10 finished jobs (6 s before there are any). It warns when the run will stop at 40 nights.
- **One job per chronicle**, Update at most every 5 minutes, Rewrite everything once an hour; cancelled and failed jobs don't count. Without PostgreSQL there is no writer, and Update answers 503 `no_worker`.
- **Quiet first.** The writer starts only after 10 minutes with no new message in that chronicle (`CHRONICLE_QUIET_MINUTES`); until then the job says "Waiting for a quiet moment". Before each call it also waits while a Storyteller reply is being written anywhere (the `ai_busy_marks` row each Storyteller generation bumps).
- **Never cut a night.** A night's lines, the answer's `max_tokens` and room for a Storyteller prompt (LM Studio's parallel slots share one context) must fit in the loaded context, or the night is split into parts that are written and joined. On "Context size has been exceeded" the job waits 30 s (`CHRONICLE_OVERFLOW_WAIT_S`) and writes the night in two halves; an answer cut off at `max_tokens` (`finish_reason: length`) is written in halves at once. A prompt is never trimmed (`generate_for_role(shrink=False)`).
- **Partial runs.** At most 40 nights per job (`CHRONICLE_MAX_NIGHTS_PER_JOB`), and a job stops after the night that passes 3600 s of writing (`CHRONICLE_MAX_SECONDS_PER_JOB`, at least 60); the status line says "40 of 63 nights; Update continues".
- **Old jobs pruned.** Finished job rows older than 90 days (`CHRONICLE_JOB_KEEP_DAYS`) are deleted once a day by the nightly scheduler; live ones are never touched.
- **Jobs in the database.** `chronicle_jobs` holds the state, so either gunicorn worker can answer a poll. A worker thread looks at queued jobs first, then waiting ones, oldest first, with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; a chronicle that isn't quiet is marked waiting and skipped, so it never holds up another chronicle, and a waiting row is looked at again after 5 s at the soonest. Each chronicle has an advisory lock; nothing is claimed while a Storyteller reply is being written; a running job whose row hasn't changed for 60 s is picked up again.
- **Errors** in the panel are short codes (`model_failed`, `context`, `no_json`, `internal`); the provider's own message goes only to the log.

## Settings and the nightly run (0.28)

Chronicle settings → The chronicle so far → Settings, for the Storyteller (creator) and site admins only (`GET`/`PUT …/chronicle/settings`; helpers and players get 403). Stored as JSON in `campaigns.chronicle_settings` (`services/chronicle_settings.py`), like `creation_settings` and `voice_settings`:

- **Night gap**, in hours (default 6, from 2 to 24): a night ends after that long with no message. The cutter, the jobs, the date-range minutes and the archive on delete all use it. A new gap cuts the nights again: only the nights it cuts differently get a new hash, so the next Update writes those again and removes the chapters that no longer exist.
- **Table talk in exports by default** (default off): an export without `with_table_talk=` follows it; the panel's switch starts from it and sends `with_table_talk=0|1` only when it differs.
- **Nightly run** (default off): see below.
- **Writer model** (default: the chronicler roles): one of the models the admin AI panel lists (each role's configured provider and model, without the small Ollama model). It is tried first, then the role's own chain. A cloud writer can only be picked by a site admin, and the panel warns that the whole chat, hidden rolls included, leaves the server. An override the AI panel no longer lists is ignored.

**The nightly run.** At `CHRONICLE_NIGHTLY_AT` (server time, default `05:00`), for up to 3 hours, the first gunicorn worker (the one `gunicorn.conf.py` `post_fork` starts the background jobs in) queues an ordinary Update for each chronicle with the switch on and new messages since its last finished Update (a partial one counts as unfinished), one chronicle at a time: the next is queued only when the previous nightly job has ended. Nightly jobs have no `requested_by`. They follow the same rules as Update: the job queue and its claim, the quiet window, the busy marker, one job per chronicle, Update every 5 minutes, and the caps. Each chronicle is tried once per night.

## Live progress

The model's answer is streamed (`stream: true` with `stream_options.include_usage`) through an `on_token` callback of the LM Studio provider; the Storyteller's replies stay non-streaming. The job keeps its progress in memory and writes it to its row at most every 1.5 s, and at each night and at the end: the night and the number of nights, tokens for the night and in total, tokens per second over the last 2 s, the phase (waiting, writing, eras, joining) and the time left, which is refined after each night and never goes up.

The panel polls the job every 1.5 s (5 s while waiting for a quiet moment, whose length the panel takes from the server) and shows "Night 3 of 12", a progress bar, "1,240 tokens · 104 tok/s" ("Reading the night…" before the first token), elapsed and time left, and Cancel. Cancel closes the stream, so LM Studio stops writing.

## Exports

- **Story** (`chronicle.md`): in short, the story so far, open threads, cast and places from the database (players' usernames only in the Storyteller's view, in brackets in the cast list), every night in short, and the Storyteller-only part. Optional `with_table_talk=1`.
- **Minutes** (`chronicle/minutes.md`, whole chronicle, `last=N` nights, or a date range since 0.26): every written night in full — summary, what happened with message numbers, who, where, threads, rolls, what the players didn't see. It is a render of the stored nights: no model call, and it says which nights it covers and how many aren't written yet.
- **PDF** (`chronicle.pdf`, `chronicle/minutes.pdf`, since 0.25): the same text in the character sheet's look, black ink on white: the sheet's double frame, the sigil at the top of every page, section headings between hairlines and diamonds, Cinzel for English headings, EB Garamond for the text and for Greek headings (in capitals without accents). `paper=a4` (default) or `paper=letter`. It is drawn from the same Markdown as the `.md` export, which is built from the stored digest and chapter JSON (or from the edit copy); only headings, paragraphs, lists, bold and italic are read from it, and nothing in the text becomes a link. In the Storyteller's view the Storyteller-only part starts on a new page. Bold uses EB Garamond Bold (a static weight-700 instance of the OFL variable font, `EBGaramond-Bold.ttf`), registered for the chronicle PDF only, so the sheet's output is unchanged. Crossed marks (`**a _b** c_`) are closed and reopened in order, and a line reportlab still refuses is printed as plain text. A heading too long for the rule is cut with an ellipsis. The look is shared with the sheet in `services/pdf_look.py` (frame, sigil, section rule, footer, fonts).
- **Minutes for a date range (0.26):** `from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD` on `minutes.md` and `minutes.pdf`, both days included, as calendar days in the Storyteller's timezone: `tz=` (an IANA name such as `Europe/Athens`), else the user's display timezone, else UTC. A night counts by the day of its first message, so a night that runs past midnight belongs to the day it started. Every date in the file is in that timezone. One date alone is open-ended: `from` alone runs to today, `to` alone from the beginning. Only written nights are rendered, with the numbers they have in the whole chronicle; nights in the range that are new or changed since the last Update (a session mark or a moved cut included) are counted once at the top ("3 nights in this range are not written yet or changed since: press Update"), and a stored night whose cut no longer exists is left out. Only the nights in the range are looked at for this, not the whole chronicle. In the other language, nights the translation lacks are counted too ("… still in Greek or not translated: press Translate"). A range wins over `last=`. Bad dates, a range that ends before it starts, or an unknown `tz` answer 400 (`bad_range`, `bad_tz`). Like all minutes, it is always the generated record, never the edit copy. The panel has From and To date pickers beside the number of nights; it sends the app's display timezone, or this device's when none is set.
- **The horror look (0.26):** `look=horror` on `chronicle.pdf` and `minutes.pdf` (the sheet look stays the default, and its output is unchanged), chosen in the panel with **Look: Sheet / Horror** beside Paper. The look of the print PDFs made for a character: a stained-paper background, a double blood-red frame with thorned corners, a few blood splatters in the margins and blood dripping from the top of the first page, dark-brown ink, blood-red headings, EB Garamond with real bold. The paper is a JPEG made once on a workstation (`backend/assets/pdf/paper-a4.jpg` and `paper-letter.jpg`, 250 dpi, about 170 KB, a light and calm paper so the text reads easily; the README there says how) and embedded once per file with its bytes unchanged (read once per paper size and kept); the backend has no browser. reportlab draws it on every page, then the frame, corners and blood as vector paths (`services/pdf_horror.py`, the same geometry as the print builder, a fixed seed per page so the file is the same every time), then the text as vector, so Greek can be selected, searched and extracted. The text keeps clear of the darkened edges, the frame and the art (70 pt at the sides, more at the top of the first page for the drips). A4 and Letter.
- **Colour or print (0.26):** `color=colour|print` on `chronicle.pdf` and `minutes.pdf`, for both looks, chosen in the panel beside Look and Paper (Colour / Print (black and white)); the default follows the look: colour for horror, print for the sheet, so a request without it gives what it always did, and the sheet in print is byte for byte the sheet as before. **Horror in print** has no paper image: a white page with the same frame, thorned corners, drips and splatters drawn in black and greys (splatters light grey, drips mid grey), black text and headings, and the same layout and margins as in colour, so it is clean and easy on ink. **The sheet in colour** is the sheet with blood-red headings, section rules and sigil (the horror look's heading red); everything else is the same.
- **`lang=`** on every export (and on `GET …/chronicle`) asks for the other language: the cached translation. The footer then adds "translated from Greek" (or English).
- Model text is never read as markup: it is escaped before it goes into Markdown. Citations of deleted messages show "(message removed)". The footer says "Written by ShadowRealms AI" with a link to the app's page on lefteros.com in the file's language (`/apps/shadowrealms-ai/` for Greek, `/en/apps/shadowrealms-ai/` for English), the one link in the file; in a PDF it is on every page with the page number. The link shows as `lefteros.com/apps/shadowrealms-ai` (English: `lefteros.com/en/apps/shadowrealms-ai`) and points to the full address; in Markdown it is `[lefteros.com/apps/shadowrealms-ai](https://lefteros.com/apps/shadowrealms-ai/)`. Both come from `FOOTER_URL`. There is no model name, server or version in the file; the PDF's producer is "ShadowRealms AI".

## The Storyteller's edit copy (0.25)

The chronicle's Storyteller and site admins can edit the story: **Edit the story** opens one Markdown box with the whole story in the Storyteller's view (without the footer), and **Save my edit** stores it with `PUT …/chronicle/edit`. Helpers read and export it but can't save (403). The generated text is never changed; the copy is kept on the digest row (`edited_markdown`, `edited_by`, `edited_at`) and survives Update and Rewrite everything.

- The story exports in the Storyteller's view (`chronicle.md`, `chronicle.pdf`) use the copy when there is one, and the footer says "edited by the Storyteller". The table talk option doesn't apply to an edited copy.
- The player view never uses it (it could hold the Storyteller's part), and neither do the minutes: they are always the generated record, whole chronicle or last N nights (Lef's decision). A translation is made from the generated text, not from the edit; the edit is for the chronicle's own language only.
- The copy remembers which digest it was based on (`edited_from_hash`). When the story is written again after the edit, the panel says so and offers **Keep my edit** (`PUT {keep: true}`) or **Start from the new one** (`DELETE`, which drops the copy and opens the new text).
- At most 200,000 characters.

## Session ended here (0.26)

The chronicle's Storyteller and site admins (not helpers, not players) can mark a message as the end of a session from the message menu (the same menu as Copy, Reply and Read this): **Session ended here**. The same button, now **Remove the end of session mark**, takes it away. Everyone in the room then sees a thin "— end of session —" line («— τέλος της συνεδρίας —») under that message.

- The marks are `chronicle_session_marks` rows (one per message). The chat loads them with one small request per chronicle (`GET …/chronicle/session-marks`), apart from the room's messages, so the chat never waits for it. Players only get marks in rooms they can read, and never the id of a row hidden from them: a mark on a hidden roll is given to them as the latest message they can see in that room at or before it, so the line still shows in the right place. Removing a mark that doesn't exist answers 404. On phones the message toolbar's buttons are 44 px.
- A mark ends the session in every room at its time, so it changes how the nights around it are cut. Those nights' hashes change, and the next Update writes them again; removing the mark puts the old cut back.

## Previously on… (0.27)

When someone opens a room after 12 hours or more away, a closable **Previously on…** card («Προηγουμένως…») shows above the chat: the nights of that room since they were last there (titles and summaries, newest last, at most 5) and the story so far in short (the digest's short version, or the start of the story so far, about 600 characters). On phones it is a bottom sheet. The scroll button in the room header shows it any time; with nothing new it shows the room's last 3 nights, and says so when there is nothing at all.

- **Jump to the start** under each night scrolls the chat to that night's first message, loading older messages until it is there, and flashes it like the reply-quote jump.
- **Read it** reads the card aloud with the user's own voices (phone or server, speed and volume as set in voice settings), even with Listen off, like "Read this". It is never read on its own. With server voices the text is made on the server from the same recap, in the narrator's voice; the browser never sends text to speak. The audio is indexed in `voice_audio` with the chronicle and room (`campaign_id`, `location_id`, no message), so only a member who can still read that room can play it, and it is deleted with the chronicle (`forget_campaign_audio`). If the recap changes while it is read (its `recap_hash`), the server answers 409 `recap_changed` and reading stops quietly.
- Closing the card is remembered per room in the browser until the next absence of 12 hours or more. On phones, a jump hides the sheet without closing it, so the header button brings it back.
- Nothing shows when there is no written story yet, when the story is held back (a room closed since the last Update), or when there are no nights in that room since the last visit.

Where it comes from: the stored player view only (chapters and digest), with no model call. The room is checked again when it is read with the viewer's readable rooms, so a room they can't read is 404; the nights and the digest are checked against what players can read now, for everyone who asks (the Storyteller sees the players' recap too). There are no usernames, no `st_*` fields, no hidden rolls (a night's jump target is never a hidden roll's id), no closed rooms and never the Storyteller's edit copy. It is in the chronicle's language, or in the viewer's language when a translation of the story is saved and up to date with it (a translation behind the story is not used).

"Last there" is a `chronicle_room_visits` row per user and room: `last_seen_at` is set when the room is opened, every 10 minutes while it stays open in a visible tab (a hidden tab sends nothing), and when the tab becomes visible again (a new absence then opens the card); when the gap since the last one is 12 hours or more, `away_since` moves to the old `last_seen_at`. The recap counts the nights that ended after `away_since`, so a reload shows the same card. The chat's own read markers (`location_reads`) are per character and move as messages are read, so they couldn't say where an absence began.

## When a chronicle is deleted

Before anything is deleted, `delete_campaign` writes `data/chronicle_archive/<id>-<name>-<time>/` (or `CHRONICLE_ARCHIVE_DIR`) with two files and an index row in `chronicle_archives`, with no model call:

- `minutes.md`: the minutes of the nights already written, with table talk;
- `transcript.md`: every chat line of every room as the Storyteller saw them (dice animations and system rows left out), hidden rolls marked.

Both start with what they cover. Admin → Archives lists them (name, Storyteller, deleted at and by, size, nights covered) with both downloads; a Storyteller whose chronicle was deleted finds the same list for 30 days under Deleted chronicles in the account menu (`/archives`). If they can't be written, the delete stops with an error and the chronicle stays; if the delete fails after they were written, they are removed again, so a retry doesn't leave two copies. Site admins can always download them; the chronicle's Storyteller (its creator) can for 30 days, whoever pressed delete (`GET /api/chronicle-archives`, `GET /api/chronicle-archives/<id>/minutes|transcript`). An archive is kept until an admin deletes it.

## API

All under `/api/campaigns/<id>/chronicle`:

| Route | What |
|---|---|
| `GET …/chronicle?view=st\|player` | the digest, the nights, the status (written, total, new messages since) and the last job |
| `GET …/chronicle/chapters` (`?key=`) | the nights |
| `GET …/chronicle/estimate` | nights to write, seconds, capped at 40, enough messages or not |
| `GET …/chronicle/estimate?scope=translate` | texts to translate and seconds |
| `POST …/chronicle/jobs` `{scope: update\|all}` or `{scope: translate, lang}` | 202 with the job; 409 while one runs (or no digest to translate); 429 with `retry_after`; 400 for the chronicle's own language |
| `GET` / `DELETE …/chronicle/jobs/<job>` | poll / cancel (cancel: Storyteller and site admins only) |
| `GET …/chronicle.md\|pdf`, `GET …/chronicle/minutes.md\|pdf` | the exports (`view`, `lang`, `last`, `with_table_talk`, `paper`, `look`, `color`; minutes also `from`, `to`, `tz`) |
| `GET` / `POST …/chronicle/session-marks` `{message_id}`, `DELETE …/chronicle/session-marks/<message>` | the session-end marks: any member reads, the Storyteller and site admins mark and unmark |
| `GET` / `PUT …/chronicle/settings` | the chronicle's settings (Storyteller and site admins): `{gap_hours, table_talk, nightly, model}`, plus the models offered, the gap range, the caps and the nightly time |
| `GET` / `PUT` / `DELETE …/chronicle/edit` | the edit copy (Storyteller and site admins): read, save `{markdown}` or `{keep: true}`, drop |
| `POST …/chronicle/recap/visit` `{location_id}` | any member, a room they can read: records the visit, returns `away_since` |
| `GET …/chronicle/recap?location_id=` (`recent=1`, `lang=`) | any member, a room they can read: `nights` (night, title, summary, dates, `first_message_id`), `so_far`, `since`, `lang`, `recap_hash` |
| `POST …/chronicle/recap/tts?lang=` `{location_id, chunk, recent, recap_hash}` | "Read it" with server voices: a speech job for one chunk of the same recap; 409 `recap_changed` |

Outside a chronicle: `GET /api/chronicle-health` (site admins: jobs and failures today, the median seconds per night, the last error code, running and queued jobs; shown in Admin → AI system as Chronicle writer, next to Voice health) and `GET /api/chronicle-archives` (with `storyteller`, `deleted_by`, `size`).

`view=player` is what players will get: no Storyteller keys, no message ids, no usernames, no NPC that no visible night names, no closed room in the places, and rooms are checked again when it is read (a room closed since the last Update drops out, and the digest is held back until the next Update).

## Data

`chronicle_chapters` (one row per night and language: the JSON, the hash, the message range), `chronicle_digests` (eras and the final digest; the edit copy in `edited_markdown`, `edited_by`, `edited_at`, `edited_from_hash`; a cached translation is a row in the other language with `translated_from`), `chronicle_jobs`, `chronicle_archives`, `chronicle_session_marks`, `ai_busy_marks`, `chronicle_room_visits` and `voice_audio.campaign_id`/`location_id` (0.27), `campaigns.language` and `campaigns.chronicle_settings` (0.28). They are created by `ensure_chronicle_tables`, `ensure_campaigns_language_column` and `ensure_campaigns_chronicle_settings_column` in `backend/database.py` with the same DDL in `init_postgresql_schema.sql`. Chapters, digests and jobs go with the chronicle.

Code: `backend/services/chronicle_digest.py` (the pure part: lines, cutting, prompts, checks, Markdown, translation texts), `backend/services/chronicle_pdf.py`, `backend/services/pdf_look.py` and `backend/services/pdf_horror.py` (the PDF), `backend/services/chronicle_jobs.py` (the database, jobs and the writer thread), `backend/services/chronicle_archive.py`, `backend/services/chronicle_settings.py`, `backend/services/chronicle_nightly.py`, `backend/routes/chronicle.py`, `frontend/src/features/chronicle/ChronicleSoFarPanel.jsx`, `ChronicleSoFarSettings.jsx`, `WriterHealthPanel.jsx`, `ChronicleArchivesPanel.jsx`, the marks in `frontend/src/features/chat/MessageActions.jsx`, and the recap in `frontend/src/features/play/PreviouslyOn.jsx` and `previouslyOn.js`.
